Campus Standard

Second pass, rebuilt against the products your users already open every day. The patterns here are borrowed on purpose: LinkedIn's card structure and warm grey ground, Instagram's feed and flat tab bar, Unstop's opportunity listings, Devfolio's hackathon page, and Y Combinator's restraint about colour.

Nothing below is invented where a convention already exists. Students shouldn't have to learn a new interface to use a campus app, and recruiters trust a layout that behaves like the tools they hire with.

Six screens

Light ground, white cards, 13–15px type, one accent reserved for primary actions. Every screen uses a flat anchored tab bar rather than the floating pill — see the note at the bottom.

One recommendation attached to a bug

Every screen here uses a flat tab bar anchored to the bottom edge instead of your floating pill. Instagram, LinkedIn, Unstop and Devfolio all do this, and it removes the entire class of bug you asked me to fix earlier: a floating bar overlays content, so every scrollable screen has to reserve clearance for it by hand, and four of yours had guessed wrong. An anchored bar occupies real layout space, so nothing can slide underneath it. It also gains about 40px of usable screen height.

If this direction is right, the next useful step is a real screen build rather than more mockups — one screen in the actual app, on a device, where type, touch targets and long Indian names get tested against reality. Say which screen and I'll port it.
