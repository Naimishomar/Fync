Fync · Direction 01 · Campus Ink

Built for the notice board, not the timeline.

Fync serves three audiences with one screen. Students want somewhere that feels like theirs. Recruiters and alumni want somewhere that looks like it can be trusted. Most campus apps pick a side and lose the other. This direction borrows from what a campus already prints — admit cards, ID lanyards, hostel notice boards, riso fest posters. Monospaced data fields and a stamped verification mark read as official. Hot riso orange and heavy display type keep it young. Same design language, both jobs done.

01

Six screens

Every screen below uses one accent, one grey family, and an 11px type floor. Data is set in monospace with tabular figures so numbers line up in columns the way they do on a result sheet.

What changed, and why it matters

WasNowWhy
6–10px body text 11px floor Below the iOS minimum. College names and timestamps were unreadable.
slate + zinc + gray slate only Three grey hues side by side make surfaces look accidentally dirty.
147 loose hexes One accent + roles Boldness spent in one place reads intentional; spent everywhere reads unfinished.
2.56:1 muted text 4.76:1 Passes WCAG AA. Also just easier to read on a phone outdoors.
Sentence-case metadata Mono data fields Reads as a record, not a caption. This is what buys recruiter trust.

One direction, six screens. If Campus Ink is close but not right, the two levers worth pulling first are the accent (drop the riso heat toward a deeper amber for a more corporate read) and the ground (paper-first instead of ink-first, which skews older and calmer). Both are token changes, not redraws.
